## Local Markdown Links

The form of absolute links is supported:

	<https://example.com/path/to>

However, local links in this form WILL NOT be supported:

	</path/to>

This is because they look like end tags and html5lib parses them as such.

Correctly supporting this would be a major undertaking.

Concept:
    step "4b" in `to_markdown` should be eliminated

    Request:
        in this step, we alter the existing dom to swap the ``TokenHR`` with (``TokenNewline``, ``TokenHR``(stripped), ``TokenNewline``)
        a better approach would be to surround the ``TokenHR`` in a special block
        
        ``TokenProtectedBlock``
        
        this could potentially be applied to ``TokenHN`` too
        
        ``TokenProtectedBlock`` would protect the contents from having their newlines adjusted
        
        for example:
    
            TokenProtectedBlock
            TokenHR
            TokenProtectedBlock
            TokenNewlines(2)
            TokenCharacters
            
        when analyzing the above for cleanup, The TokenNewlines(2) would consolidate to a TokenNewslines(1) but not be able to alter the TokenProtectedBlock

    Analysis:

        I agree with the goal in [TODO #2]. A horizontal rule should carry its line-break requirements through whitespace cleanup. I’d keep that behavior specific to TokenHR for now. A generic TokenProtectedBlock would add a new abstraction for one demonstrated case.
        
        Step 4 already treats TokenHR specially when adjusting surrounding whitespace. Step 4b then walks the entire token list to split the rule into a leading newline, ---, and a trailing newline. That late split keeps the rule’s own line breaks out of the earlier cleanup, while preserving the blockquote prefix on each side. The order matters. For example, before<hr>after currently produces before\n\n---\nafter. Losing the blank line before --- could turn before into a Setext heading. [transformer.py (line 1286)]
        
        I’d make TokenHR explicitly own those line breaks and expand it once the spacing decisions for its neighbors are settled. I’d first lock down the current output for rules next to plain text, paragraphs, other rules, and code blocks, as well as rules at blockquote boundaries. Those cases would tell us whether step 4b can be folded into step 4 without changing either the rendered Markdown or the tokens returned by adapt().
        
        I’d leave headings alone until there’s a concrete case for the same treatment. TokenHNStart owns the heading prefix, while the heading text arrives in separate tokens and the surrounding block markers handle its spacing. Removing step 4b is primarily a maintainability improvement. It saves one linear pass and one temporary list, and the speedup is unmeasured. No files changed.
